How to compare a saved weather forecast with the actual temperature

24 November 2026

A forecast checked after the weather has happened may already have been replaced by a newer forecast. A useful comparison needs a copy saved beforehand, its capture time and the later observation for the same place and hour.

Follow one hour

Compare saved Langley and Halifax temperature forecasts with station observations. Choose a station and forecast source, then use Inspect a receipt to select a capture. Under Follow one weather hour, choose a local station time. The table shows the available saved predictions for that hour and their errors once an eligible reading arrives.

The receipt stores UTC times, while the station-time column helps you read them locally. Check both when a date crosses midnight. Langley Regional and Halifax Stanfield are airport measurements; they do not describe every neighbourhood’s temperature.

A small, reproducible example

The first saved Langley NOAA GFS receipt was captured at 04:43:08 UTC on 19 November 2026. It predicted 3.8 °C for 06:00 UTC that day, which was 10 p.m. on 18 November in Langley.

In the saved Langley observation file reviewed on 24 November, that hour has an unflagged reading of 4.2 °C. Forecast minus observation is therefore −0.4 °C: this prediction was four-tenths of a degree too cool. Its absolute error is 0.4 °C. One comparison illustrates the calculation; it cannot establish which provider is generally better.

The model initialization in that receipt is 18:00 UTC on 18 November. That is separate from the time CoolCode downloaded it. The tool measures lead time from capture to the valid hour so the stored evidence remains clear.

Compare like with like

Use the lead-time, month and local-time filters when reading aggregate accuracy. A forecast made an hour ahead and one made four days ahead are different tests. Successive receipts for the same valid hour count as separate predictions, so the matched-pair total is not a count of independent weather days.

Signed bias averages forecast minus observation; positive values mean predictions were warmer on average. Mean absolute error averages the magnitudes of those differences. Always read the number of matched pairs and the source availability beside the result.

Why some hours have no score

An hour in the future cannot have an observation yet. A later hour can also lack a numerical, unflagged station reading. Those cases stay unscored. The forecast archive began on 19 November 2026 and cannot reconstruct a forecast that was never captured. A later download is not substituted for a missed historical receipt.

The forecast series comes from NOAA GFS through Open-Meteo’s Single Runs API; the measurements come from ECCC hourly climate observations. Check the tool’s availability messages before comparing providers. For daily historical station records and normals, explore the Canadian Weather Archive.
