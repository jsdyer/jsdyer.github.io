How many sockeye returned to the Adams River?

28 November 2026

Fisheries and Oceans Canada estimated 346,191 naturally spawning adult sockeye in the Adams River (Salmon Arm) late run in 2022. Four years earlier, the estimate for that same run and measure was 535,564. If you remember a crowded river one autumn and only a few fish another, the years you choose make a difference.

Compare years on the same four-year cycle

The lower Adams River is known for a dominant sockeye run every four years. In DFO’s June 1, 2026 data edition, three successive dominant-cycle years recorded 707,023 natural adult spawners in 2014, 535,564 in 2018 and 346,191 in 2022. The 2022 estimate was about 35% below 2018. Those estimates describe the size of the two runs. Explaining the difference would require other evidence.

DFO recorded just 354 on the same measure in 2024, two years after 2022. Comparing those two figures mixes different points in the cycle. To repeat the like-for-like comparison, open the Adams River late-run sockeye record. Follow its chart link, choose Natural adult spawners and read every fourth year in the observation table.

Why might two people quote different numbers for 2024?

For the late run, DFO estimated 354 natural adult spawners and 1,373 total natural spawners in 2024. The total includes jacks, the males that mature early. Both numbers belong to population 46239 at waterbody feature 220. The early-summer sockeye record at that very same feature has its own population ID, 46238. Check the run name and measure before comparing a figure you saw elsewhere.

The June 2026 edition ends in 2024 for this late-run population. The fall 2026 return came after publication, so 2022 is the latest dominant year you can compare in this edition. To check the underlying rows, select population 46239 and feature 220 in the original DFO all-areas CSV, then read the analysis year and the natural adult spawners column. The NuSEDS catalogue provides the data dictionary.

When can you see the run?

DFO identifies late October as the usual spawning period at the Adams River near Squilax. BC Parks identifies 2026 and 2030 as dominant-cycle years. That calendar helps you choose an autumn. Actual numbers and timing vary. For a particular visit, open Tsútswecw Park in Park Ready for published notices and entrance information. Check the official park page and DFO’s salmon-viewing guide before you go.
