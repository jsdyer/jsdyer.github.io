How to check Langley building permits and development applications

24 November 2026

A construction site can have several records: a rezoning application, a development permit and a building permit. Their identifiers and dates answer different questions. Start by separating the municipality and the type of record you need.

Choose the right list

Search Langley development applications and Township building permits. The application explorer combines City of Langley and Township of Langley records. The building-permit history covers the Township’s active list; it does not provide a City building-permit archive.

  1. For a planning proposal, search its street or application number. Use the municipality and community filters to narrow the results. The Willoughby preset is a useful starting point.
  2. Open the record and read the municipality’s original status wording and linked documents. A broad filter group is only a shortcut through those source labels.
  3. For a building permit, use the Building permits section and search the street, folder number, type or description. A single folder may cover several civic addresses.

A dated example from Willoughby

The snapshot reviewed on 24 November 2026 lists development permit DP100823 at 7743 and 7787 200 Street. Its source application date is 18 January 2016, while the tracker’s first observation is 18 November 2026. Those dates describe different events: the application predates this archive by years.

Compare the saved record with the Township’s original DP100823 application. The source description discusses a seniors-oriented and multi-family community with commercial space. Read the actual documents to understand which phase or permission a particular record concerns. A listing’s status does not establish whether construction has started.

What does a disappearing permit mean?

The permit archive began with its first capture on 19 November 2026 UTC. A folder present in that baseline may have been listed long before the tracker existed. Later checks can establish when a folder was last observed and the first successful check that no longer contained it.

That observation interval is not an exact removal time or a final-inspection approval date. A missing folder alone does not prove a project was completed, cancelled or abandoned. Check the Township’s current building-permit list and its explanation of building-permit activity information before drawing a conclusion.

Keep the evidence with the result

Save the application or folder number, the official link and the snapshot retrieval date. Export the filtered rows if you need to compare them later. Search again after a later successful collection to see what the tracker actually observed changing; the first capture cannot reconstruct earlier history.
