What a Canadian connection in the Offshore Leaks Database means

27 November 2026

Filter the Pandora Papers to Canada and the Offshore Leaks Database shows zero entities, 723 officers and 214 addresses. How can all three be true? The country filter counts each kind of record separately. A person or company with a Canadian connection can be listed as an officer of a company incorporated elsewhere. Open the Canada results in the Pandora Papers.

Where tax havens fit

An offshore company is incorporated outside its owners’ home jurisdiction. A tax haven can attract that business through low taxes on some income or assets. Rules that limit public disclosure can also make it hard to trace who ultimately controls a company. A company can be incorporated in one place, managed from a second and licensed to serve customers in a third. Each location answers a different question. Read ICIJ’s introduction to offshore finance.

To investigate a jurisdiction such as Malta, first ask which files were available to the reporters. The Paradise Papers include a snapshot from Malta’s own corporate registry. That alone makes Maltese companies easier to find in this collection. Measuring tax actually paid would require accounts, tax findings or other financial evidence.

Follow one connection

  1. Under Categories, open Officers and choose a record. ICIJ uses “officer” for a person or a company with a recorded role. Follow the link to an entity and read the exact role, such as director, shareholder or beneficial owner.
  2. On the entity page, read Registered in for the jurisdiction of incorporation. Linked countries can come from a contact address. Some Pandora sources also used nationality, residence or country of birth.
  3. Read the source name and the year its records cover. Save the entity’s ICIJ link and any dated relationship you intend to describe. When the relationship dates are blank, its duration is unknown.

A Vancouver mailing address can connect a record to Canada. It cannot establish who owns the property at that address or where the company paid tax. Similar names can refer to different people or companies. Read how ICIJ assigns countries and handles duplicate records.

The original Offshore Leaks providers’ records extend through 2010, Mossack Fonseca’s Panama Papers through 2015, and the Bahamas corporate registry through early 2016. The Paradise Papers combine Appleby records through 2014 with corporate registries through 2016. Pandora’s cutoff varies by provider, with some records reaching 2020. These differences explain why the counts cannot measure a rise or fall in Canadian offshore activity. See ICIJ’s sources for each investigation.

Compare a historical registry entry with a licence

In the Paradise Papers, the Malta corporate registry records BML Group Limited’s incorporation on 21 October 2004 and lists Betsson AB as a shareholder. The relationship dates are blank and the registry snapshot ends in 2016. Local Maltese businesses appear in this collection. Follow the BML Group and Betsson records in the atlas or read ICIJ’s original entry.

For a later question about that business, Betsson’s 2025 annual report lists BML Group Ltd under corporate identity number C 34836. On 27 November 2026 the Malta Gaming Authority listed BML Group Limited as licensed under consumer gaming licence MGA/B2C/108/2004. Each source dates a different fact. Betsson’s 2025 annual report · Malta Gaming Authority licence.

Offshore structures have lawful uses. The public database shows recorded relationships and omits bank accounts and financial transactions. A claim about tax evasion or other wrongdoing requires independent evidence about the conduct, such as a published court finding. For a question about a gambling operator’s licence or a regulatory decision, use the legal name, activity, jurisdiction and decision date in Gambling Regulator Watch.
