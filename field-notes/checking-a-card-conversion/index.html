How to check a foreign-currency card charge

25 November 2026

A USD 100 purchase appears on a Canadian statement as CAD 143.50. How much did the conversion add? Start with three figures: the original price and currency, the final posted amount in Canadian dollars, and a reference exchange rate with a date.

Work through the receipt

  1. Use the final posted transaction. A pending authorization can change. Record the purchase and posting dates if they differ.
  2. Open the currency purchase comparison and select the reference date. Check the rate direction. A quote in Canadian dollars per US dollar can be multiplied by the original US price.
  3. Compare against the posted total. If a separate fee appears elsewhere on the statement, include it once when calculating your total cost.

Try the calculation

Using an illustrative rate of CAD 1.40 per USD, USD 100 converts to CAD 140.00. The statement amount exceeds that reference by CAD 3.50. Divide 3.50 by 140.00 and multiply by 100 to get 2.50%. The effective rate paid was CAD 1.435 per USD.

If an additional CAD 2.00 fee was charged separately, the total becomes CAD 145.50. The difference from the same reference becomes CAD 5.50, or about 3.93%. Keep both calculations if you want to distinguish conversion from a separately listed charge.

Read the date on the rate

The Bank of Canada publishes daily indicative exchange rates on business days. For a weekend purchase, record which observation you used. Issuer processing dates and exchange-rate sources can also differ. The calculated percentage describes the difference from your chosen reference. The card agreement and statement supply the evidence for an issuer fee.
