When is an 18-pack of eggs cheaper than a dozen?

28 November 2026

Multiply the price of a dozen by 1.5. If 12 eggs cost $4.80, an 18-pack at $7.20 has exactly the same price per egg. Below $7.20, the 18-pack is cheaper per egg. Compare cartons with the same egg size and any production standard you care about.

Which carton should go in the basket?

Suppose the shelf labels say $4.80 for 12 and $6.75 for 18. These are example prices. The dozen costs 40¢ per egg. The 18-pack costs 37.5¢ per egg. What to buy depends on how many eggs you expect to use.

  • Need 12? The dozen costs $1.95 less at checkout. Buying 18 gives you six extra eggs for that $1.95.
  • Need 18? One 18-pack costs $6.75. Two dozens cost $9.60 and leave six eggs for another meal.
  • Need 36? The package sizes finally line up exactly. Two 18-packs cost $13.50 and three dozens cost $14.40, a saving of 90¢ for the same number of eggs.

Put your own shelf prices into the egg carton calculator. It opens with both cartons and a target of 12 eggs. Change the target to 18 or 36 to compare checkout totals and leftovers.

How do those prices compare with a Canadian average?

Statistics Canada’s September 2026 average was $5.02 for a dozen in Ontario and $4.83 across Canada. At the Ontario rate, an 18-pack would cost $7.53. That is a conversion from the published dozen price. The source reports a monthly average for the dozen, and a store sets its own prices for each carton size.

For a purchase this week, use the smaller carton’s shelf price in the formula. To compare it with the published series, open Ontario egg prices by month or choose your province in the Canadian egg-price comparison. Both use the same one-dozen product in Statistics Canada table 18-10-0245-01.
