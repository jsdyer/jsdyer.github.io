How to check an unfamiliar online store in Canada

25 November 2026

A shop offers the item you want and claims a Canadian address. Before entering payment details, write down its domain name, the business name on its returns page and one specific claim you can check, such as “operating since 2012.” A domain check needs only the hostname. Keep checkout links, order numbers and sign-in pages private.

Compare the dates

Open the Canadian domain history example. CIRA’s registry record lists a February 1998 registration for cira.ca. The example has an independently checkable registration date. Replace the example hostname with the store’s domain, then look for the earliest available homepage capture and an archived contact page.

Suppose a store claims twenty years of sales. If a dated homepage from five years ago shows a different business, ask the seller when the current business took over the domain. An old registration can predate the current shop. Archives also miss pages and years. A quiet period leaves that part of the timeline unresolved. Check the underlying dates in CIRA’s .ca lookup.

Check who will take your money

  1. Compare the seller’s name on the checkout page, invoice and return policy. Look for a usable return address, shipping terms and a way to resolve complaints.
  2. If the seller claims federal incorporation, use the Canadian corporation lookup with the legal name. Compare the result with the identity the website claims. Provincial incorporation and trading names can require a different registry. A real company’s identity can also be copied.
  3. Contact the business using a phone number or address you obtained independently. Ask about the specific item, return location or advertised warranty. Keep the answer with a dated copy of the offer. If the identity or return arrangement is still unclear, hold off on paying.

The Government of Canada lists the contact, payment and return details shoppers should check in its online shopping guide.
