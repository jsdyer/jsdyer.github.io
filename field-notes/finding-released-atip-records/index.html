How to find previously released federal ATIP records

24 November 2026

Before drafting a new access-to-information request, check whether someone has already asked for similar records. A completed-request summary can provide a subject, a date range, an institution and a request number to investigate. The summary is not the released document package.

Start with a subject, then narrow the search

Search completed federal ATIP summaries for “real-time rail”. This example searches the phrase used for Canada’s payment system project; it is a starting query, not a claim that every relevant record uses that wording.

  1. Try an exact phrase in quotation marks, then try related wording without quotes. The tool searches bilingual summaries, request numbers and dataset comments.
  2. Use the institution filter once the results suggest which department holds relevant material. Similar subjects can appear under several institutions.
  3. Inspect the completion year and outcome. The year a request was completed is not necessarily the year the documents were written.
  4. Open promising summaries and save the request numbers and institutions. An individual number is much more useful for follow-up than a screenshot of a search count.

Read what the summary actually says

Look for the requested date range, named offices and document types. A briefing note, a correspondence search and a contract request can share a keyword while describing very different records. Read both language fields if one is unclear.

A page count describes the source’s reported response, not how much of it you will find useful. A completed request may have no releasable pages. A disclosure outcome also needs its context; the tool’s filters help you find candidates but do not interpret the contents of a release.

Follow the official record

Use Request a copy on the selected summary to reach the official lookup or prepare a note for the institution. Keep the request number, institution and summary together. Use the Government of Canada’s completed ATIP search to check the current listing, and follow the institution’s instructions for obtaining the package.

If the saved CSV contains a summary that the live portal cannot find, retain its citation and check with the named institution. The two publications can differ. If nothing matches, broaden the wording or consider a new request; an empty search cannot establish that the underlying records do not exist.

Keep a reproducible shortlist

Export this page CSV exports the currently displayed result page. Saving records across searches lets you export a shortlist. Neither action exports every matching result unless you have explicitly included them. The export carries the bilingual summaries, official links and snapshot date.

The ATIP snapshot downloads and provenance document the searchable files and their coverage. The Treasury Board dataset covers published completed federal summaries; provincial and municipal requests use separate systems, and some federal summaries are published elsewhere or excluded.
