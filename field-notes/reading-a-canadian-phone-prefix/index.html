What a Canadian phone prefix tells you about a call

25 November 2026

A number on your screen looks local. Its first digits can lead you to a published telephone-number assignment. To verify the person who called, you need a separate step.

Read a block assignment with seven digits

  1. Open the Canadian phone prefix lookup. Enter 942-945-4 to use a Toronto numbering-block example without entering a subscriber number.
  2. Read the prefix row for 942-945. The first three digits are the area code and the next three select an exchange code. The row identifies the published exchange, province and company holding the prefix. Open it for its assignment details.
  3. Read the additional line for 942-945-4xxx. The seventh digit selects a block of 1,000 possible numbers. In the CNA edition of 25 November 2026, the prefix holder was Bell Canada and the listed block holder was Rogers Communications Canada Inc. (Cable). Compare the displayed edition and status when you repeat the lookup. A missing block entry means the edition only supplies the prefix-level evidence for your search.

To explore a place, open the Halifax or Vancouver directory. A city can use several area codes. The exchange identifies the area recorded in the numbering plan. The Canadian Numbering Administrator publishes the block editions and status definitions.

Verify a call separately

A subscriber can keep a number when changing providers, and a caller can change the number shown on your display. Someone claiming to be your bank could show a local exchange or even the bank’s own number. End the call and use the number printed on your card or statement to contact the bank. The CRTC explains number portability and caller ID spoofing.
